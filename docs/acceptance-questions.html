What does the "Top Stock Input" field on a receipt line mean?

ANSWEREDrelevance 0.9688.7stop: Stock generation.txt

The “Top Stock Input” field on a receipt line indicates the stock generation status for that line.

  • Value 0 (default when a line is added): The line has not been generated into stock. No stock movement exists, expected items are not consumed, and the goods cannot be used in logistics flows.
  • Value 1 (after generation): The line has been generated into stock. Stock entry movements are created, associated expected items are consumed, and the goods become available for logistics flows (e.g., picking, sampling).

Key points:

  • Only lines with Top Stock Input = 0 can be generated.
  • Clicking Generate Stock updates the field from 0 → 1 for the selected lines.
  • Once generated (Top Stock Input = 1), the receipt line cannot be modified or deleted.

Why is stock generation a separate step after creating receipt lines?

ANSWEREDrelevance 0.9708.1stop: Stock generation.txt

Stock generation is a separate step to control when goods become official inventory and to ensure proper system records are created. Specifically:

  • Receipt lines are not immediately available in stock after being created; they exist only in REL_DAT with Stock Input = 0 and no movements in MVT_DAT.
  • Stock generation is required to:
  • Create stock records in STK_DAT.
  • Record entry movements in MVT_DAT.
  • Consume expected quantities if linked to a supplier order.
  • Make the storage media available in logistics flows (picking, sampling, etc.).
  • Keeping it separate allows lines to be modified or deleted safely before any stock or movement is created.
  • The Top Stock Input field on each receipt line indicates whether stock has been generated (not generated vs. generated), preventing premature use of goods in logistics.
  • It also ensures the receiving dock location on the receipt header is confirmed before inventory is made available.

To generate a receipt from an expected reception in Speed WMS, follow these steps and rules:

Access and scope

  • On the Expected Items screen, select one or more expected lines.
  • Open the contextual (right-click) menu and choose Generate Receipt.
  • Only one purchase order (PO) can be processed at a time. Regardless of how many expected lines you select, the entire PO will be brought to the processing screen.
  • Processing multiple POs simultaneously is not supported; repeat the operation per PO.

Receipt line population

  • All lines from the selected PO are displayed.
  • Fields highlighted in yellow are editable.

Managing expected quantities and flags

  • For each expected line:
  • Check the quantity to be returned to stock and modify it if needed.
  • Tick the TG (To Generate) box to mark the line for processing in the receipt.
  • For variable weight items, enter the total weight for the expected quantity.
  • Lines already received are automatically ticked as G (Generated) and cannot be modified.
  • Partial receipt: If you enter a quantity lower than expected, the line will be split, keeping the remainder for future processing.

Validation and inheritance

  • Click the Validation button once all lines are reviewed and adjusted.
  • The system may present different validation options depending on configuration.
  • If additional information was saved on the expected items and inheritance or inheritance mapping is activated, these details will automatically appear in the generated receipt.

What happens next

  • Upon validation, receipt lines are created. At this stage, stock is not yet generated.
  • Proceed to stock generation via the standard reception workflow (open the Receipt form, go to Lines, and select lines for stock generation).

Additional business rules

  • One expected line corresponds to one planned receipt quantity; partial consumption is allowed.
  • Expected lines remain editable until consumption (stock entry) starts.
  • Once stock entry is validated, expected quantities are reduced.

Orders are put into a wave as follows:

Automatic wave assignment (default and recommended)

  • Use the Waves tab in the preparation window to get a global view of all waves in progress, including:
  • Status of each wave
  • Orders associated with each wave

  • Tables: OPE_DAT, VAG_DAT

What's the difference between the absolute rate and the proximity rate in the availability calculation?

ANSWEREDrelevance 0.9808.9stop: Availability calculation.txt

Difference between Absolute and Proximity Availability Rates

  • Absolute Availability Rate (TXDA)
  • Scope: Entire warehouse.
  • Purpose: Gives an overall view of whether the total stock in the warehouse can cover the total need of the selected orders.
  • Calculation basis: Considers the total need of the orders.
  • Storage: Updated on orders and lines (OPE_TXDA, OPL_TXDA) after calculation.
  • Proximity Availability Rate (TXDP)
  • Scope: Advanced preparation (proximity) zone.
  • Purpose: Indicates whether the preparation zone is sufficiently stocked or if transfers (replenishments) to this zone are required before picking.

What does the restocking calculation take into account?

ANSWEREDrelevance 0.9838.7stop: Restocking calculation method.txt

The restocking calculation takes into account:

  • Replenishment unit (e.g., pallet, box) used for replenishment
  • Maximum quantity allowed at the picking location (Max Quantity at Picking)
  • Replenishment threshold that triggers a refill if stock falls below it
  • Quantity already allocated in orders currently in preparation
  • Current available quantity at the picking location
  • Picking samplings not yet validated
  • REA (replenishment) not yet validated
  • Existing replenishments and transfers to/from the picking area

Restocking logic:

  • If the item is present in orders in preparation and the current picking quantity is below the max at picking, a replenishment is generated with Quantity to replenish = Max at picking – Quantity currently at picking.
  • If the item is not in orders in preparation:
  • If current picking quantity < threshold, generate a replenishment.
  • If current picking quantity ≥ threshold, no replenishment is generated.

Calculation formulas provided:

  • Need = AvailableQtyPicking – sum(PickingSamplingsNotValidated) + sum(REA_NotValidated) – ReplenishmentThreshold
  • QtyToReplenish = PicSamplQty + MaxPickingQty – PicAvailableQty – ReplQty – QtyTransfToPick – QtyTransfFromPick

Notes:

  • Adjustments can be made depending on saturation, adding threshold quantity or max picking quantity.

What are the statuses a support can have after reception, and what moves it between them?

ANSWEREDrelevance 0.98310.9stop: The different statuses of a support.txt

After reception, a storage support can have these statuses and transitions:

  • 01 – Support in place
  • Meaning: The support is already located in its storage location. This can represent both “already in place” at reception time (often when locations are not managed) or “now in place” after a move.
  • How it’s reached:
  • Immediately after reception if the support is considered already in its location.
  • After a planned move is executed (see 03 → 01).
  • Via Storage Validation, which automatically moves the support to its planned location and sets status to 01.
  • 02 – Location to be assigned
  • Meaning: The support is on the reception platform; destination location not yet assigned.
  • How it changes:
  • When a destination location is assigned (manually in the Storage tab or via automatic location search), the status changes to 03.
  • 03 – Movement to be performed
  • Meaning: A planned storage location exists, but the support hasn’t been moved yet.
  • How it changes:
  • Once the support is moved to the planned location, status changes from 03 to 01. The initial location is replaced with the planned location, and the movement is recorded in Speed history.
  • Storage Validation also performs this move automatically and sets the status to 01.

Key points:

  • Status 01 indicates the support is stored and available for further operations like order picking.
  • Status 02 is temporary until a location is assigned.
  • Assigning a destination (manual or automatic) drives 02 → 03; executing the move (manually or via Storage Validation) drives 03 → 01.

How does automatic location search work when storing a support?

ANSWEREDrelevance 0.98412.7stop: Automatic search location.txt

Automatic location search assigns a planned storage location to each support (pallet, bin) during receiving/storage, based on predefined rules, so operators don’t have to pick locations manually.

How it’s triggered

  • From the Storage tab of a receipt: select the relevant receipt lines and either right‑click → Launch automatic search or click the Location search button.
  • In RF storage: if automatic location search is enabled, after you scan the support the system suggests a location; the operator can override or re‑search.

How the system decides the location

  • Speed WMS uses Location Search rules configured under Settings → Locations → Location Search.
  • Each rule consists of sequences that are executed in order. Available methods:
  1. Search location in structure – finds the first empty slot in a storage area.
  2. Search as close as possible to picking – finds the first empty slot near the item’s picking location.
  3. Search by complement – finds a location already occupied by the same item reference.
  4. Fixed search – returns a predetermined fixed location.
  5. Free search – custom method configurable only by BK Systems teams.
  • The system optimizes the proposal based on rule criteria such as:
  • Zone type
  • Stock constraints
  • Item characteristics
  • Capacity limitations

System behavior and statuses

  • If a destination location is found and assigned, the support status changes to 03 – Movement to be performed (the support now has a planned location but hasn’t been moved yet).
  • If no suitable location is found (e.g., storage zone full or criteria not met), the Search completed box remains unchecked and manual intervention may be required.
  • When the support is moved and stored in the planned location (RF steps: scan support, scan storage location, confirm), the status changes from 03 to 01 – Support in place, the planned location becomes the current location, and the movement is recorded in history.

Additional notes

  • In the Storage tab, the current location, the item’s reference picking layout, the intended destination (blank by default in administrative mode), and the support status are displayed.
  • Goods can also be stored directly in their picking location using Initialize picking to set that as the destination.

How are DFAB and DLUO dates calculated automatically from a batch at L'Oréal?

ANSWEREDrelevance 0.98117.1stop: L’ORÉAL – Batch Decoding & Automatic DFAB,DLUO Calculation.txt

At L’Oréal, DFAB and DLUO are computed by decoding the batch (LOT) and then applying a simple offset rule:

  1. Decoding paths
  • If the batch starts with “+”:
  • Decode the year using AYR (from the TRANS table). Example: K → 2013.
  • Extract the day-of-year (JJJ) from the numeric part (001–365/366), applying leap-year logic.
  • DFAB is the calendar date corresponding to that day-of-year in the decoded year.
  • If the batch does NOT start with “+” and matches ACT_ALPHA2:
  • Decode the year using BYR (from the TRANS table). Example in the document: J → 2012.
  • Extract the month using AMO (from the TRANS table). Example: 7 → July.
  • DFAB is set to the first day of that month in the decoded year (e.g., 01 July 2012).
  • If the batch cannot be decoded (does not start with “+”, does not match ACT_ALPHA2, or required letters are missing):
  • No automatic decoding. DFAB and DLUO must be entered manually. DLC remains manual.
  1. DLUO rule
  • Once DFAB is determined, DLUO = DFAB + ART_NUM1 days (ART_NUM1 is the article’s configured number of days between DFAB and DLUO).
  1. Examples from the specification
  • Case 1 (batch “+K0060”, ART_NUM1 = 10):
  • AYR: K → 2013; JJJ: 006 → 6th day of 2013.
  • DFAB: 06/01/2013.
  • DLUO: 06/01/2013 + 10 days = 16/01/2013.
  • Case 2 (batch “2YJ700”, ART_NUM1 = 10):
  • From TRANS: J → 2012; 7 → July.
  • DFAB: 01/07/2012.
  • DLUO: 01/07/2012 + 10 days = 11/07/2012.

Notes

  • The same decoding logic is also applied in the Cycle Counting module (PE884), mirroring PE882.
  • Leap-year logic must be respected when interpreting JJJ (001–365/366).

Here’s how to close a GRN (receipt) in Speed WMS, along with key preconditions and effects:

How to close a receipt (GRN)

  • Open the Receipt Management screen.
  • Select the target receipt.
  • Choose one of the following:
  • Contextual menu → Close Receipt, or
  • Speed WMS command bar → Close Receipt button.
  • Confirm the closure action.

Preconditions before closure

  • All receipt lines have been correctly recorded.
  • Quantities and item references are validated.
  • Required documents or media have been attached.
  • No further modifications are expected.
  • Best practice: Close only after full verification; reopening is not possible.

What happens after closure

  • The receipt is frozen and cannot be modified.
  • No new receipt lines can be added; existing lines cannot be edited or deleted.
  • No new media or attachments can be added.
  • The receipt remains accessible in read-only/advisory mode for consultation.
  • Attached documents can still be viewed and edited/printed.
  • Closed receipts remain available for reporting and audit.

System integration

  • Feedback to upstream/n+1 systems (e.g., ERP) generally occurs after receipt closure.
  • Only closed receipts should be considered final for downstream processing.
